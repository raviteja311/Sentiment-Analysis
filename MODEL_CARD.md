# Model Card: Tweet Sentiment Classification

Four models for three-class sentiment classification of short English social
media text. This card covers all four; they share a dataset, a preprocessing
path and a label scheme, and differ only in architecture.

**Labels:** `0 = negative`, `1 = neutral`, `2 = positive`. That ordering is fixed
by the dataset and by every artifact here; it is recorded in `src/config.py` and
in `models/roberta/config.json`.

## Models

| Key | Architecture | Parameters | Framework |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lr` | TF-IDF (1-2 grams, 10,000 features) + multinomial Logistic Regression, `saga`, `class_weight="balanced"` | ~30k coefficients | scikit-learn 1.7.2 |
| `lstm` | GloVe-Twitter Embedding(10k x 100) → Bi-LSTM(128) → Bi-LSTM(64) → Dense | 1.42M | Keras 3.12 |
| `gru` | GloVe-Twitter Embedding(10k x 100) → Bi-GRU(128) → Bi-GRU(64) → Dense | 1.32M | Keras 3.12 |
| `roberta` | Fine-tuned `cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment` | 125M | Transformers 4.57 |

Keyed as `bert` until the name was corrected; that key still resolves to
`roberta` as a deprecated alias.

## Intended use

Exploratory and educational sentiment scoring of short English text, and as a
reference implementation of a multi-model serving setup. Suitable for
aggregate-level analysis where individual errors wash out.

### Out of scope

- **Decisions about individual people** - moderation, hiring, credit, discipline.
  A model that is right about seven tweets in ten is not a basis for acting on
  any single one.
- **Languages other than English**, and text much longer than a tweet. The
  recurrent models truncate at 80 tokens, the transformer at 128.
- **Domains far from Twitter** - reviews, clinical notes, legal text. TweetEval
  is drawn from Twitter and carries its vocabulary and conventions.
- **Anything treating the confidence score as a calibrated probability.** See
  Limitations.

## Training data

[`cardiffnlp/tweet_eval`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cardiffnlp/tweet_eval),
`sentiment` config.

| Split | Examples |
|---|---|
| train | 45,615 |
| validation | 2,000 |
| test | 12,284 |

Training-split class balance: neutral 20,673 (45.3%), positive 17,849 (39.1%),
negative 7,093 (15.5%).

Preprocessing (identical at training and inference, in
`src/utils/preprocessing.py`): lowercase, URLs → `<url>`, `@mentions` → `<user>`,
hashtags keep the word and drop the `#`, emoji are demojized to text, whitespace
collapsed.

### Reproducing

```bash
pip install -r requirements/train.txt
make train-lr && make train-lstm && make train-gru && make train-roberta
make evaluate
```

Training is seeded (`SEED = 42`). The recurrent models initialise their
embeddings from GloVe Twitter vectors (93.0% of the 10k vocabulary is covered),
downloaded on demand; set `SequenceConfig.pretrained_embeddings = None` to train
them from scratch, which costs about 0.03 macro F1 and 0.05 positive-class F1. The Logistic Regression retrain reproduced its
predecessor's probabilities on sample inputs to within 0.0001; the transformer
retrain landed within 0.001 macro F1 of the original run's best epoch (0.7815
against 0.7806).

## Limitations

**The recurrent models sit between the baseline and the transformer.** Bi-LSTM
and Bi-GRU beat TF-IDF by about 6 points of accuracy and remain within 0.4
points of each other; any claim that they differ meaningfully is not supported
by these numbers. Both trail the transformer by ~7 points of macro F1.

**The positive class is still their weak spot**, at 0.575 and 0.582 F1 against
0.67-0.68 for neutral, though far less so than before pretrained embeddings
(0.522 and 0.530). Positive recall is 0.51-0.52: roughly half of positive tweets
are still read as neutral. The transformer does not share this failure mode
(0.700 F1, 0.732 recall).

**Why it happens, and what did not fix it.** The test split carries a very
different class balance from training - 32.3% negative and 19.3% positive
against 15.5% and 39.1% - so the models are deployed under a substantial label
shift. Three interventions were measured and rejected: balanced class weights
(worse overall), a decision bias fitted on validation (+0.024 positive F1 but
costs accuracy and loses macro F1 on the GRU), and EM label-shift correction
(catastrophic - it estimated the test prior as 77% negative and halved macro
F1). What did work was giving the models pretrained meaning rather than
re-weighting their existing predictions.

**Class imbalance is only partly addressed, and weighting it made things
worse.** (Measured before the switch to pretrained embeddings; the conclusion
was not retested afterwards.) The Logistic Regression uses `class_weight="balanced"`; the neural
models deliberately do not. That looked like an oversight, so it was measured:
both were retrained with balanced weights (negative 2.14, neutral 0.74,
positive 0.85) and evaluated on test.

| | LSTM | GRU |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | 0.6168 → 0.5961 | 0.6175 → 0.5798 |
| Macro F1 | 0.5921 → 0.5838 | 0.5952 → 0.5727 |
| F1 negative | 0.5942 → 0.6242 | 0.5980 → 0.6215 |
| F1 neutral | 0.6598 → 0.5948 | 0.6573 → 0.5521 |
| F1 positive | 0.5223 → 0.5324 | 0.5303 → 0.5446 |

Weighting buys 2-3 points on negative, the rare class, and about 1 on positive,
and pays 6-10 points on neutral, the 45% majority. Both models lose accuracy and
macro F1. The shipped artifacts are therefore unweighted; `SequenceConfig.class_weight`
turns it back on for anyone who would rather have the negative-class recall.

Note also that the smallest class is not the worst-scoring one. Positive is, for
all three non-transformer models, despite having two and a half times more
training examples than negative - so imbalance was never the main problem here.

**Confidence scores are calibrated, within limits.** They were raw softmax
outputs; the transformer averaged 0.917 confidence at 70.8% accuracy. Temperature
scaling, fitted on validation and measured on test, cut its expected calibration
error from 0.2092 to 0.0925, and the Bi-LSTM's from 0.0466 to 0.0147. The Bi-GRU
is left uncalibrated: after retraining with pretrained embeddings its ECE is
already 0.0220 and the fitted temperature lost ground on held-out validation
folds. A residual ECE near 0.09 on the transformer is still not nothing:
read a 0.95 as "usually right", not as 95 correct calls in 100. The linear
baseline is deliberately left uncalibrated - see the README - because the only
temperature that helps it on validation harms it on test.

**Test-set scores are the honest ones.** An earlier version of this project's
documentation claimed 85-95% accuracy across these models. Those figures
corresponded to nothing measurable; the table above supersedes them.

**Temporal and topical drift.** TweetEval predates current slang, events and
platform conventions. Performance on recent text will be lower than the table
suggests.

## Where the weights live

The large weights are hosted on the Hugging Face Hub at
[RAVITEJA311/sentiment-analysis-models](https://huggingface.co/RAVITEJA311/sentiment-analysis-models),
not in the git repository. `make fetch-weights` downloads them. The Logistic
Regression pipeline and the tokenizers are small enough to stay in git.

## Provenance

Every score here is regenerated by `make evaluate` from the committed artifacts,
and `reports/metrics/<model>.json` records the hyperparameters, split sizes and
class distribution behind it. Numbers in this card should never be edited by
hand - regenerate them.
